## SECTION 1: Total Knee Arthroplasty (TKA) Cases

---

### Case TKA-01
**Post-Op Day:** 3
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported mild generalized knee swelling and stiffness. Pain rated 4/10, primarily around the incision site. No systemic symptoms. ROM (range of motion) significantly reduced.
**Medications:** Acetaminophen 1000mg PRN, Enoxaparin 40mg daily (DVT prophylaxis).
**Assessment:** Expected early post-operative inflammatory response. Normal findings for Day 3.
**Diagnosis:** Normal post-operative inflammation.
**Action Taken:** Continued physical therapy protocol. Advised ice and elevation. No escalation required.
**Outcome:** Patient recovered uneventfully. Full ROM restored by week 6.

---

### Case TKA-02
**Post-Op Day:** 8
**Patient Presentation:** Patient called in reporting a new onset of unilateral lower extremity edema and a sensation of tightness and heaviness in the calf. Pain in the calf rated 7/10, described as deep and throbbing. Knee incision was healing normally with no signs of redness or drainage.
**Medications:** Warfarin 5mg daily (switched from Enoxaparin on Day 5), Acetaminophen 500mg PRN.
**Assessment:** Unilateral calf swelling and deep throbbing pain on Day 8 post-TKA is Deep Vein Thrombosis until proven otherwise. Post-operative immobility and hypercoagulable state are classic risk factors. Patient is on anticoagulation but Warfarin takes several days to reach therapeutic INR.
**Diagnosis:** Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT), left lower extremity.
**Action Taken:** Immediate duplex ultrasound ordered. Confirmed DVT. Warfarin dose adjusted after checking INR. Hematology consulted.
**Outcome:** Clot resolved over 3 months with therapeutic anticoagulation. No PE occurred.

---

### Case TKA-03
**Post-Op Day:** 5
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported taking 400mg Ibuprofen every 6 hours for breakthrough pain in addition to prescribed Warfarin. No new musculoskeletal symptoms. Mild nausea reported.
**Medications:** Warfarin 5mg daily, Ibuprofen 400mg (self-administered OTC), Acetaminophen 500mg PRN.
**Assessment:** Concurrent NSAID and Warfarin use is a serious pharmacological interaction. NSAIDs inhibit platelet aggregation and can significantly potentiate Warfarin's anticoagulant effect, increasing the risk of GI hemorrhage, hematoma at the surgical site, and elevated INR. Nausea may be early GI irritation.
**Diagnosis:** Drug Interaction Risk — NSAID-potentiated anticoagulant hemorrhage risk.
**Action Taken:** Instructed patient to discontinue Ibuprofen immediately. INR checked — found to be supratherapeutic at 3.8. Warfarin dose held for one day and reduced. Switched breakthrough pain management to Acetaminophen 1000mg.
**Outcome:** INR normalized within 48 hours. No bleeding event occurred.

### Case TKA-04
**Post-Op Day:** 12
**Patient Presentation:** Patient presented with erythema (redness), localized warmth, and purulent drainage from the inferior aspect of the incision. Low-grade fever of 38.1°C. Surgical site appeared mildly dehisced at one corner.
**Medications:** Acetaminophen 1000mg PRN, Aspirin 81mg daily.
**Assessment:** Wound erythema, warmth, purulent drainage, and fever are classic signs of surgical site infection (SSI). Must rule out deep infection vs. superficial wound infection.
**Diagnosis:** Surgical Site Infection (SSI) — Superficial.
**Action Taken:** Wound swab culture sent. Empiric oral Cephalexin started. Patient instructed on wound care. Follow-up in 48 hours for culture results.
**Outcome:** Culture grew Staphylococcus aureus. Responded well to Cephalexin. Wound healed by day 28.

### Case TKA-05
**Post-Op Day:** 18
**Patient Presentation:** Patient called reporting sudden onset shortness of breath, pleuritic chest pain (sharp, worse on inspiration), and heart rate of 102 bpm. Patient had previously had mild calf pain that was not escalated at Day 14 due to patient refusal of ultrasound.
**Medications:** Warfarin 5mg daily.
**Assessment:** Acute respiratory symptoms (dyspnea, pleuritic pain, tachycardia) in a post-TKA patient with a history of unreported calf pain is a Pulmonary Embolism until proven otherwise.
**Diagnosis:** Pulmonary Embolism (PE), likely secondary to undiagnosed DVT.
**Action Taken:** Called emergency services. Patient admitted. CT pulmonary angiography confirmed bilateral PE. Therapeutic anticoagulation initiated with IV heparin bridge.
**Outcome:** Patient survived and was discharged on Rivaroxaban. This case highlighted the importance of not deferring DVT workup.

**Outcome:** Uneventful recovery. Discharged from post-op monitoring at week 8.

### Case TKA-07
**Post-Op Day:** 21
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported severe, constant pain (9/10) in the knee, accompanied by high fever (39.2°C), significant joint effusion, and warmth. Patient felt systemically unwell with chills and rigors.
**Medications:** Warfarin 5mg daily, Acetaminophen 1000mg PRN.
**Assessment:** High fever, rigors, severe joint pain with effusion at 3 weeks post-TKA suggests periprosthetic joint infection (PJI), which is a surgical emergency. Must rule out septic arthritis.
**Diagnosis:** Periprosthetic Joint Infection (PJI) — High suspicion.
**Action Taken:** Emergent orthopedic surgery consultation. Joint aspiration performed, fluid sent for cell count, culture and sensitivity. IV antibiotics started empirically. Patient admitted.
**Outcome:** Culture confirmed Streptococcal PJI. Underwent irrigation and debridement (I&D). Recovered with 6-week IV antibiotic course.

### Case TKA-08
**Post-Op Day:** 10
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported numbness and tingling along the lateral aspect of the foot and lower leg, with foot drop. No pain. No swelling in the calf.
**Medications:** Acetaminophen 1000mg PRN, Aspirin 81mg daily.
**Assessment:** Foot drop and lateral lower leg numbness post-TKA is consistent with common peroneal nerve injury, a known complication of knee surgery, particularly in patients with pre-existing valgus deformity or tourniquet use.
**Diagnosis:** Common Peroneal Nerve Palsy (post-surgical).
**Action Taken:** Emergent orthopaedic review. MRI of the knee ordered. Referred to neurology. Splint applied to prevent foot drop contracture. Physical therapy modified.
**Outcome:** Partial neurological recovery over 4 months. Patient regained functional walking ability.

### Case TKA-09
**Post-Op Day:** 30
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported inability to straighten knee fully (extension deficit of 15 degrees) and marked stiffness. Pain manageable at 3/10. No signs of infection. Patient had been non-compliant with physical therapy.
**Medications:** Meloxicam 15mg daily, Acetaminophen 500mg PRN.
**Assessment:** Extension deficit and stiffness at 30 days post-TKA in a non-compliant patient is consistent with developing arthrofibrosis (excessive scar tissue formation).
**Diagnosis:** Early Arthrofibrosis / Stiffness Post-TKA.
**Action Taken:** Intensive physiotherapy reinstatement. Patient counselled on compliance. Manipulation Under Anesthesia (MUA) discussed as a potential option if ROM does not improve by 6 weeks.
**Outcome:** With aggressive PT compliance, patient achieved 110 degrees flexion and full extension by week 10.

### Case TKA-10
**Post-Op Day:** 14
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported bilateral leg swelling (both legs equally swollen), no calf tenderness, no fever. Patient had resumed a sedentary lifestyle.
**Medications:** Warfarin 5mg daily, Furosemide 20mg daily (pre-existing heart failure).
**Assessment:** Bilateral symmetric lower extremity edema in a patient with pre-existing heart failure and Furosemide use is more consistent with decompensated heart failure or medication-related edema rather than DVT (DVT is typically unilateral). However, must not rule out bilateral DVT.
**Diagnosis:** Probable decompensated congestive heart failure (CHF) exacerbation. DVT lower on differential given bilateral presentation.
**Action Taken:** Bilateral duplex ultrasound performed to rule out DVT (negative). Cardiology consulted for CHF management. Furosemide dose adjusted.
**Outcome:** Edema resolved with optimized diuresis. No thromboembolic event.

## SECTION 2: Coronary Artery Bypass Graft (CABG) Cases

---

### Case CABG-01
**Post-Op Day:** 2
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported mild, expected chest wall soreness at the sternotomy site, rated 4/10. Slight drainage from the leg harvest site (saphenous vein graft). No fever. Heart rhythm was regular on monitoring.
**Medications:** Aspirin 81mg daily, Metoprolol 25mg twice daily, Atorvastatin 40mg daily.
**Assessment:** Mild sternotomy pain and minor harvest site drainage are expected findings in the early post-CABG period.
**Diagnosis:** Normal early post-CABG recovery.
**Action Taken:** Continued standard post-operative monitoring. Harvest site dressed. Pain management with Acetaminophen.
**Outcome:** Uneventful recovery.

---

### Case CABG-02
**Post-Op Day:** 4
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported palpitations, described as a "fluttering" sensation in the chest, with associated mild dizziness and fatigue. Heart rate on monitoring was irregularly irregular at 110 bpm. No chest pain at rest.
**Medications:** Aspirin 81mg daily, Metoprolol 12.5mg twice daily (dose was recently reduced due to bradycardia concern), Amiodarone 200mg daily.
**Assessment:** Irregularly irregular heart rate, palpitations, dizziness, and fatigue post-CABG is Post-Operative Atrial Fibrillation (POAF) until proven otherwise. POAF is the most common complication after cardiac surgery, occurring in up to 30-40% of patients. It peaks between Days 2-5 post-operatively.
**Diagnosis:** Post-Operative Atrial Fibrillation (POAF).
**Action Taken:** 12-lead ECG confirmed AF with rapid ventricular response. Cardiology consulted. Amiodarone IV loading dose given. Rate control achieved. Anticoagulation initiated with heparin bridge to Warfarin to reduce stroke risk.
**Outcome:** Patient converted to sinus rhythm on Day 6. Discharged on oral Amiodarone and Warfarin.

---

### Case CABG-03
**Post-Op Day:** 7
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported redness, warmth, and a small amount of straw-colored discharge from the lower end of the sternal wound. Temperature 38.3°C. White cell count elevated.
**Medications:** Aspirin 81mg daily, Metoprolol 25mg twice daily, Atorvastatin 40mg daily.
**Assessment:** Sternal wound erythema, warmth, discharge, and fever post-CABG raises concern for sternal wound infection (SWI), ranging from superficial cellulitis to deep sternal wound infection or mediastinitis.
**Diagnosis:** Sternal Wound Infection (SWI) — Superficial, rule out deep infection.
**Action Taken:** Wound swab and blood cultures taken. CT chest ordered to assess for deep sternal involvement. Empiric IV antibiotics started (Vancomycin). CT showed no mediastinal involvement.
**Outcome:** Superficial SWI. Responded to antibiotics and wound care. No reoperation required.

---

### Case CABG-04
**Post-Op Day:** 3
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported increasing breathlessness at rest, oxygen saturation dropping to 91% on room air, and crackles heard bilaterally at lung bases on auscultation. Chest X-ray showed bilateral infiltrates.
**Medications:** Aspirin 81mg daily, Furosemide 40mg daily.
**Assessment:** Post-CABG pulmonary edema is common due to the inflammatory response from cardiopulmonary bypass. Bilateral infiltrates, decreasing O2 saturation, and basal crackles are classic signs.
**Diagnosis:** Post-operative pulmonary edema / pleural effusions.
**Action Taken:** Increased Furosemide dose. Supplemental oxygen via face mask. Patient encouraged to use incentive spirometry. Repeat CXR ordered.
**Outcome:** Oxygenation improved by Day 5 with diuresis. No intubation required.

---

### Case CABG-05
**Post-Op Day:** 10
**Patient Presentation:** Patient became confused and disoriented overnight. Family reported inability to recognize family members. Neurological examination revealed right arm weakness and expressive aphasia (difficulty finding words).
**Medications:** Aspirin 81mg daily, Warfarin 5mg daily, Metoprolol 25mg twice daily.
**Assessment:** New-onset confusion, focal neurological deficit (arm weakness, aphasia) in a post-CABG patient is a stroke until proven otherwise. Peri-operative stroke is a devastating complication of cardiac surgery.
**Diagnosis:** Acute Ischemic Stroke (post-CABG).
**Action Taken:** Emergent neurology and neurosurgery activation. CT head performed — ischemic infarct in left MCA territory confirmed. Thrombolysis contraindicated given recent surgery. Patient admitted to stroke unit.
**Outcome:** Significant long-term neurological deficit. Transferred to inpatient rehabilitation.

### Case CABG-06

**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported feeling generally well. Mild sternotomy discomfort (2/10). Ambulating with assistance. Good appetite returned. Heart rhythm regular on Telemetry.
**Medications:** Aspirin 81mg daily, Metoprolol 25mg twice daily, Atorvastatin 40mg daily, Lisinopril 5mg daily.
**Assessment:** Patient is progressing as expected. All parameters within normal limits.
**Diagnosis:** Normal post-CABG recovery trajectory.
**Action Taken:** Step-down from cardiac monitoring. Discharge planning initiated. Cardiac rehabilitation referral made.
**Outcome:** Discharged on Day 7. Excellent outcome.

### Case CABG-07
**Post-Op Day:** 12
**Patient Presentation:** Patient developed progressively worsening shortness of breath, pleuritic chest pain, and low-grade fever. Heart rate 95 bpm. ECG showed sinus tachycardia with diffuse ST elevation and PR depression.
**Medications:** Aspirin 81mg daily, Metoprolol 25mg twice daily.
**Assessment:** Diffuse ST elevation, PR depression, pleuritic chest pain, and fever at 2 weeks post-cardiac surgery is classic for post-cardiac injury syndrome (PCIS), also known as Dressler's Syndrome.
**Diagnosis:** Post-Cardiac Injury Syndrome (Dressler's Syndrome).
**Action Taken:** Echo performed to rule out tamponade (small pericardial effusion found, not hemodynamically significant). High-dose Aspirin and Colchicine started.
**Outcome:** Symptoms resolved over 3 weeks with anti-inflammatory therapy.

---

### Case CABG-08
**Post-Op Day:** 1
**Patient Presentation:** Patient's urine output dropped significantly in the ICU. Creatinine rising rapidly from baseline 1.1 to 2.4 mg/dL within 24 hours post-surgery. Patient appeared fluid overloaded.
**Medications:** Furosemide 40mg IV, Aspirin 81mg daily.
**Assessment:** Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) is a common complication of cardiac surgery due to cardiopulmonary bypass, nephrotoxic contrast agents, and hypoperfusion. Rising creatinine and oliguria confirms AKI.
**Diagnosis:** Acute Kidney Injury (AKI), Stage 2 (KDIGO criteria).
**Action Taken:** Nephrology consulted. All nephrotoxic medications held. IV fluid management optimized. Renal replacement therapy (RRT) discussed but deferred pending trends.
**Outcome:** Creatinine peaked at 3.1 mg/dL and then trended down. No dialysis required. Discharged with close nephrology follow-up.

### Case CABG-09
**Post-Op Day:** 8
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported feeling anxious and having racing thoughts. Heart rate on Telemetry showed short runs of Ventricular Tachycardia (VT), lasting 5-8 beats, self-terminating. Patient on Amiodarone for rate control of a previous AF episode.
**Medications:** Amiodarone 200mg daily, Metoprolol 25mg twice daily, Potassium 40mEq daily.
**Assessment:** Non-sustained VT (NSVT) in the post-CABG period can be related to myocardial irritability from the surgery, electrolyte imbalances (especially hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia), or ischemia from a graft occlusion.
**Diagnosis:** Non-Sustained Ventricular Tachycardia (NSVT), electrolyte imbalance suspected.
**Action Taken:** Electrolytes checked — Potassium 3.2 mEq/L (low), Magnesium 1.7 mg/dL (borderline low). IV Potassium and Magnesium replenished. Amiodarone continued. Cardiology review for VT protocol.
**Outcome:** NSVT resolved following electrolyte correction. No further events.

### Case CABG-10
**Post-Op Day:** 6
**Patient Presentation:** Patient reported significant leg swelling and tenderness in the thigh of the leg used for saphenous vein harvest. No respiratory symptoms. Heart rate 80 bpm.
**Medications:** Aspirin 81mg daily, Warfarin 5mg daily, Metoprolol 25mg twice daily.
**Assessment:** Thigh swelling and tenderness in the harvest leg post-CABG raises concern for DVT in the proximal femoral vein, which is a higher-risk thrombus than a calf DVT due to greater PE potential.
**Diagnosis:** Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) — Femoral vein, harvest leg.
**Action Taken:** Duplex ultrasound confirmed proximal DVT in the left femoral vein. Warfarin dose adjusted. INR checked (sub-therapeutic at 1.5). Heparin bridge initiated until therapeutic INR achieved.
**Outcome:** PE did not occur. Anticoagulation continued for 6 months given provoked proximal DVT.
